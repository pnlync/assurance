Life Protection Model

Pricing, experience analysis, Solvency II and IFRS 17 for a synthetic Irish term-assurance book · Technical note · Tom Zhang · 2026

Question. How should an Irish life insurer price a term-assurance book, monitor emerging mortality and lapse experience, and translate assumption changes into profitability, IFRS 17 earnings and Solvency II capital?
Status of the data. Real: CMI 00 series mortality tables (IFoA), CSO Irish Life Tables No. 17, EIOPA euro risk-free curves at 31 December 2022–2025, six unisex market quotes from bonkers.ie (September 2026). Synthetic or illustrative: the 50,000 policies, the 2023–25 experience (simulated from a hidden "truth" basis), and the lapse, expense and commission assumptions. The work demonstrates method and reasoning; it is not a calibrated view of any insurer.

1. Summary

  1. Unisex pricing creates sex-mix risk. Rates are priced on a pooled margin for 60% male new business. At the priced rate a 35-year-old man earns about 4% against the 10% target; if the mix reaches 70% male, the 50-year-old non-smoker cell earns 7.1%.
  2. Fixed costs dominate small policies. With the €60 fee, a €50k level-term policy has a margin of −54% and break-even is near €177k; a fee of about €150 would flatten margins across sizes. The only IFRS 17 onerous groups are these small-cover, young cells.
  3. Mortality experience is not yet credible; lapse experience is. 75 deaths (A/E 115%, 95% CI 89%–141%, Z = 0.26) move mortality by only +4%; 12,624 lapses (A/E 121% ± 2%) are adopted in full. Brokers lapse at 133% of basis, direct business at 93%.
  4. The 2025 assumption review raises Solvency II BEL by €6.7m, cuts the CSM by €6.1m (−16%) and leaves current premiums at 96.6% of the new requirement; 2026 rates rise 2–8%.
  5. The 2027 risk-margin reform cuts the risk margin at issue by 36% (level term 38%, mortgage protection 35%) and raises day-one own funds from €23.7m to €33.0m.

2. Products, book and timeline

Level term assurance (LTA) pays a fixed sum on death within 20 years. Mortgage protection (MP) pays the outstanding balance of a 20-year repayment mortgage at 4%, S_t = SA·(1 − v^(20−t)) / (1 − v^20); Irish law generally requires it on owner-occupied mortgages (Consumer Credit Act 1995, s.126). Neither has a surrender value.

4. Methods

4.1 Cash-flow engine

Annual steps. In policy year t: premium, commission and expenses at t; death (probability q_t) paid with claim expense at t+1; survivors lapse (w_t) at t+1. In-force ℓ_{t+1} = ℓ_t(1 − q_t)(1 − w_t). The value of one policy in force at t, positive = liability:

V_t = −P + comm_t + exp_t + v_t · [ q_t (S_t + CE_t) + (1 − q_t)(1 − w_t) V_{t+1} ], V_20 = 0

One backward pass gives V at every duration, which Solvency II needs for SCR run-off and IFRS 17 for later valuations. The whole book (50,000 × 20) is valued in about 0.02 s with numpy.

4.2 Pricing and profit testing

Reserves are prospective on the reserving basis, floored at zero. Profit vector and margin:

Pr_t = (_tV + P − comm_t − exp_t)(1 + i) − q_t(S_t + CE_t) − (1 − q_t)(1 − w_t) _{t+1}V Π_t = ℓ_t Pr_t margin = Σ Π_t (1+RDR)^−(t+1) / Σ ℓ_t P (1+RDR)^−t

Unisex rates. Since 21 Dec 2012 EU insurers may not price by sex (CJEU C-236/09). For each cell (product × smoker × age) the rate solves a pooled margin of 10%: [m·NPV_M + (1−m)·NPV_F] / [m·EPV_M + (1−m)·EPV_F], m = 60% male. Premium = rate × SA/1000 + €60.

4.3 Experience study

Each policy-year draws death then lapse from the truth basis. The study sees only the data and basis_2022: A/E by count, amount and lapse across nine cuts; 95% intervals A/E ± 1.96√A/E (amount basis uses Σ q(1−q)S²); limited-fluctuation credibility Z = min(1, √(A/1082.41)); new multiplier old × (1 + Z(A/E − 1)).

4.4 Solvency II

BEL = Σ V on the EIOPA curve (no VA), full expenses. Per-policy stresses at every future time, floored at 0: mortality ×1.15; lapse up ×1.5 (only where V < 0), down −min(50%, 20pp) (only where V > 0), mass 40% of −V; expenses ×1.10 and inflation +1pp; catastrophe +0.0015 for 12 months. SCR_life(j) = √(xᵀCx) with the Art. 136 correlations. Risk margin:

RM_current = 6% Σ SCR(j) DF(j+1) RM_2027 = 4.75% Σ max(0.96^j, 0.5) SCR(j) DF(j+1) RM_2027 / RM_current = (19/24) Σ π_j max(0.96^j, 0.5), π_j ∝ SCR(j) DF(j+1) ⇒ ratio ∈ [0.396, 0.792]

4.5 IFRS 17 (general measurement model)

  • Grouping. Portfolios LTA and MP, cohort 2023; cells smoker × age band × SA band; G1 onerous if BE + RA > 0, G2 if still profitable under q×1.15 and w×1.5, else G3. Sex is not split (para 20: the difference exists only because law constrains pricing; permitted, not required; not by analogy).
  • RA. 10,000 scenarios of correlated mean-one lognormal multipliers on mortality, lapse and attributable expenses, each with its 99.5th percentile equal to the Solvency II stress; RA = 75th percentile − mean of the group PV. Values are exact via two model points per (product, sex, smoker, age), because every cash flow is affine in SA.
  • CSM. CSM₀ = max(0, −FCF₀); accretion at the locked-in (31 Dec 2022) forward rate; future-service changes measured at locked-in rates adjust the CSM; release by coverage units (expected SA in force, undiscounted). Rate changes go to insurance finance expense, never to the CSM.

4.6 Analysis of change

Each year changes one thing at a time and revalues: S0 opening → S1 expected roll-forward (identity S1 = (S0 − X)/v − EC) → S2 actual deaths → S3 actual lapses → S4 new assumptions → S5 year-end curve. The same steps run for Solvency II BEL (with overhead), IFRS 17 BE (without) at current and at locked-in rates, the RA at four points, and the CSM.

5.2 Experience 2023–25

The study recovers the lapse truth (A/E 121% against 120%) and points in the right direction on mortality (115% against 110%) but can adopt only a quarter of it. The unrecognised excess reappears as claims experience losses (€1.6m in each of 2024 and 2025). At 25–35 deaths a year, full credibility would take decades — why insurers use reinsurer and industry data.

6. Validation

  • Golden values from an independent implementation for two test policies: premium, ℓ, V, reserves, profit vector, NPV, IRR, payback, all six stress losses, SCR, BEL, risk margin, CSM, coverage units and the Monte Carlo RA (within 5% of a 200,000-scenario reference).
  • Excel single-policy model in live formulas (fixture and real basis), evaluated formula by formula in the tests; largest difference to Python ≈ 1e-12.
  • Identities: forward PV = backward V₀; in-force conservation; each BEL, RA and CSM walk reconciles to closing; RM ratio equals its identity and lies within [0.396, 0.792]; Σ CSM releases = CSM₀ + accretion; model points equal seriatim values; zero shocks give zero capital.
  • Experience study: with truth = basis, A/E inside its 95% interval in ≥ 17 of 20 seeds; the study code is tested never to read the truth file.
  • 60 automated tests; every output rebuilds with one command.

7. Limitations and next steps

Limitations. Synthetic book; CMI 00 is UK insured-lives experience from 1999–2002 with a judgement-based level; illustrative lapse, expense and commission assumptions; annual steps; life-underwriting SCR only (no assets, market, counterparty or operational risk, no tax); Monte Carlo RA covers level uncertainty only and group RAs are summed; IFRS 17 measurement and CSM roll-forward only (no revenue presentation or loss-component allocation); one cohort; simulated experience.

Next steps. Use reinsurer or industry data (e.g. SOA ILEC relativities) to strengthen the mortality basis; model lapse by channel with commission clawback; add assets and market risk for a full solvency ratio; extend to a savings or participating product (variable fee approach, options and guarantees); a Hong Kong RBC comparison.
